# Week 12 Pre-Read: The Reluctant Deliverer
## Exodus 3:1-4:17

---

### Characters

**Moses** — Born to Hebrew slaves during the time when Pharaoh had ordered every Hebrew baby boy thrown into the Nile. His mother hid him for three months, then placed him in a basket among the reeds. Pharaoh's daughter found him and adopted him. Moses grew up in Pharaoh's palace, educated in all the wisdom of Egypt, mighty in words and deeds. But he knew he was Hebrew. At age forty, he saw an Egyptian beating one of his people. He killed the Egyptian and hid the body. When the murder became known, Pharaoh sought to execute him. Moses fled to Midian.

For the next forty years, he lived as a shepherd in the wilderness—tending sheep that were not even his own, but his father-in-law's. The prince of Egypt became a fugitive in the desert. The man educated in all the wisdom of the ancient world spent four decades talking to sheep. When God appeared to him at the burning bush, Moses was eighty years old.

**Jethro** — Also called Reuel, a priest of Midian. When Moses fled Egypt, he came to a well in Midian where Jethro's seven daughters were being harassed by shepherds. Moses defended them. Jethro gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as a wife, and Moses became part of his household, tending his flocks for forty years.

**Aaron** — Moses' older brother, still living in Egypt among the Hebrew slaves. He would become central to the story of the Exodus as Moses' spokesman and as Israel's first high priest.

**Mount Horeb** — Also called Mount Sinai. The "mountain of God" in the wilderness of Midian, where Moses would later receive the Law and where God would make His covenant with Israel. This remote place in the desert would become the most sacred ground in Israel's history.

---

### Context

Four hundred years had passed since Joseph brought his family to Egypt. What began as a place of refuge had become a place of bondage. The Israelites had multiplied greatly, and a Pharaoh arose "who did not know Joseph." Fearing their numbers, Egypt enslaved them—setting taskmasters over them, forcing them to make bricks, afflicting them with heavy burdens. The people cried out to God in their suffering.

God heard their groaning. He remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—the promise that their descendants would inherit the land of Canaan. The time had come to act.

While tending his father-in-law's flock near Mount Horeb, Moses saw something extraordinary: a bush burning with fire, yet not consumed. When he turned to look, God called to him from the bush: "Moses, Moses!" God told him to remove his sandals, for the ground was holy. Then God declared: "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." Moses hid his face, afraid to look at God.

God told Moses He had seen the affliction of His people and heard their cry. He was sending Moses to Pharaoh to bring the Israelites out of Egypt.

Moses responded with objection after objection. "Who am I that I should go?" God promised to be with him. "What is Your name?" God revealed Himself as "I AM WHO I AM"—the self-existent One, the eternal God. "What if they do not believe me?" God gave him miraculous signs. "I am not eloquent—I am slow of speech." God reminded Moses that He made the human mouth. Finally Moses simply begged: "Please send someone else." At this, God's anger burned against Moses—but even then, He provided Aaron to speak on Moses' behalf.

The passage begins with Moses at the burning bush, face to face with the God who would use him to deliver a nation.
