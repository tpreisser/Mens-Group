# Week 6 Pre-Read: The Friend Who Gave Up His Throne
## 1 Samuel 18:1-4, 20:1-42

---

### Characters

**David** — The youngest of Jesse's eight sons, a shepherd from Bethlehem. The prophet Samuel had secretly anointed him as the future king of Israel while he was still a boy. The Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward. He first entered Saul's court as a harpist, summoned to play music that would soothe the king when a tormenting spirit troubled him. Then David killed Goliath. In a single day, the unknown shepherd became the most celebrated man in Israel.

**Jonathan** — Saul's eldest son and heir to the throne. He was already a proven warrior and a man of remarkable faith. In 1 Samuel 14, Jonathan and his armor-bearer alone attacked a Philistine garrison, trusting that nothing could hinder the LORD from saving by many or by few. God gave them victory that day. Jonathan was everything a crown prince should be—brave, capable, and loved by the people. By every human measure, he should have been the next king.

**Saul** — Israel's first king, anointed by Samuel at God's command. He stood a head taller than any other man in Israel. But Saul disobeyed the LORD twice—first by offering a sacrifice that only a priest should offer, then by failing to fully carry out God's command against the Amalekites. Because of this, God rejected Saul as king. The Spirit of the LORD departed from him, and a tormenting spirit came upon him instead. Samuel told Saul that the kingdom would be torn from him and given to a neighbor who was better than he. Saul remained on the throne, but he was a king without a future.

---

### Context

After David killed Goliath, everything changed. Saul brought David into his household permanently. David succeeded in every mission Saul gave him and was promoted over the men of war. The people loved him.

Then came the song. As the army returned from battle, women came out singing and dancing: "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." The comparison enraged Saul. From that day, Saul eyed David with suspicion. The next day, while David played the harp, Saul hurled a spear at him—twice. David escaped both times.

Saul recognized what was happening. The LORD was with David. The LORD had departed from Saul. The very thing Samuel prophesied was unfolding before his eyes. Saul became afraid of David and began plotting his death, even using his own daughters as bait to get David killed by the Philistines.

Jonathan saw all of this. He knew his father had been rejected. He knew David had been chosen. And he knew what that meant for his own future—he would never be king. The throne that should have been his inheritance belonged to David.

This is where our passages begin—with Jonathan's response to the man who would take his throne.
