# Week 1 Pre-Read: The Crash After the Win
## 1 Kings 19:1-18

---

### Characters

**Elijah** — His name means "My God is Yahweh." A prophet from Tishbe in Gilead who appeared suddenly during Israel's darkest spiritual era. He had spent the previous three years in hiding, sustained miraculously by God—first by ravens at a brook, then by a widow in Zarephath where he raised her son from the dead. He is the man who just called fire down from heaven.

**Ahab** — King of the northern kingdom of Israel. Scripture says he did more evil than any Israelite king before him. He built a temple and altar to Baal in Samaria and allowed his wife to systematically persecute the prophets of the LORD.

**Jezebel** — Ahab's wife, a Phoenician princess from Sidon. Her father Ethbaal was both king and priest of Baal. She didn't just permit Baal worship—she funded it, feeding 450 prophets of Baal and 400 prophets of Asherah at her royal table while hunting down and killing the prophets of Yahweh. Her threats were not empty.

**The 450 Prophets of Baal** — These were not innocent men. They were false prophets who served a false god, leading Israel into idolatry and away from the LORD who had delivered them from Egypt and made covenant with them. Under Jezebel's sponsorship, they had become the official religious leaders of the nation while true prophets of Yahweh were hunted and killed. They stood in direct opposition to God and actively turned His people toward worship of a deity that did not exist.

---

### Context

Israel had been in severe drought for over three years. This was God calling His people back to Himself—demonstrating that Baal, whom they worshiped as the storm god who controlled rain and fertility, had no power at all. Only the LORD gives and withholds rain. Only the LORD sustains life.

Then came Mount Carmel. Elijah stood alone against the 450 prophets of Baal before all Israel. The challenge: each side prepares a sacrifice, but neither lights the fire. The God who answers by fire is the true God.

The prophets of Baal cried out from morning until evening. They danced. They cut themselves until blood poured out. Nothing. Baal could not answer because Baal was not real.

Elijah rebuilt the LORD's altar, drenched it with water three times, and prayed a simple prayer. Fire fell from heaven and consumed everything—the offering, the wood, the stones, even the water. The people fell on their faces declaring, "The LORD, he is God; the LORD, he is God!" The false prophets who had led Israel astray were executed in accordance with God's command in the Law (Deuteronomy 13:1-5)—the righteous judgment for those who turn God's people toward false gods. Then Elijah prayed and the rain came, ending three years of drought as God restored what He had withheld.

The hand of the LORD came upon Elijah, and he outran Ahab's chariot seventeen miles to Jezreel.

Total victory. God had revealed Himself. His people had returned to Him. The false prophets were judged.

This is where chapter 19 begins—immediately after.
