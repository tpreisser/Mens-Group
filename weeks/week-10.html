Week 10

Pre-Reading Context

David — The youngest of Jesse's eight sons, a shepherd from Bethlehem whom the prophet Samuel had secretly anointed as Israel's future king while Saul still reigned. From the day of his anointing, the Spirit of the LORD came upon David. He first entered Saul's court as a musician to soothe the king when a tormenting spirit troubled him. Then he killed Goliath. Then he became commander of Saul's armies, winning victory after victory. The people sang of him: "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." That song ignited Saul's jealousy into murderous rage. David had been running for his life ever since—living in caves, moving from wilderness to wilderness, gathering a band of men who had joined him in exile.

Saul — Israel's first king, anointed by Samuel at God's command. But Saul had disobeyed the LORD twice in matters of sacrifice and holy war. Because of this, God rejected Saul as king, and the Spirit of the LORD departed from him. Samuel told Saul the kingdom would be torn from him and given to a neighbor who was better than he. Now Saul watched that prophecy unfold before his eyes. He knew David was the chosen one. Rather than submit to God's plan, Saul determined to kill David—throwing spears at him in the palace, sending soldiers to his house, hunting him across the wilderness with thousands of men.

David's Men — A band of about six hundred who had gathered to David during his years as a fugitive. Scripture describes them as men in distress, in debt, and bitter in soul. David became their captain. They lived with him in the wilderness, sharing his danger and his caves.

The Wilderness of En Gedi — An oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea, surrounded by barren desert. A canyon runs westward from the sea, fed by a spring that creates an unexpected paradise of waterfalls and vegetation in the midst of desolation. The rocky hillsides are honeycombed with caves. The area was known as "the Crags of the Wild Goats" for the ibex that made their home in its cliffs. It was remote, defensible, and full of hiding places—exactly what a fugitive needed.

Discussion Questions

  • David had the opportunity to take what was rightfully his by force. When have you been tempted to take a shortcut to something you knew was eventually coming?
  • David felt convicted just for cutting Saul's robe—even though Saul was trying to kill him. What does that level of integrity reveal about how David saw authority?
  • Saul wept, acknowledged David was right, and then went back to hunting him later. When has doing the right thing not changed your circumstances?
  • David had been anointed as a teenager but didn't become king until he was 30. What does it take to stay faithful when the timeline doesn't make sense?
  • "I won't touch the Lord's anointed." What boundaries have you set for yourself even when breaking them would be easier?
