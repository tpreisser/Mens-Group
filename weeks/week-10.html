Week 10

Pre-Reading Context

David — The youngest of Jesse's eight sons, a shepherd from Bethlehem whom the prophet Samuel had secretly anointed as Israel's future king while Saul still reigned. From the day of his anointing, the Spirit of the LORD came upon David. He first entered Saul's court as a musician to soothe the king when a tormenting spirit troubled him. Then he killed Goliath. Then he became commander of Saul's armies, winning victory after victory. The people sang of him: "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." That song ignited Saul's jealousy into murderous rage. David had been running for his life ever since—living in caves, moving from wilderness to wilderness, gathering a band of men who had joined him in exile.

Saul — Israel's first king, anointed by Samuel at God's command. But Saul had disobeyed the LORD twice in matters of sacrifice and holy war. Because of this, God rejected Saul as king, and the Spirit of the LORD departed from him. Samuel told Saul the kingdom would be torn from him and given to a neighbor who was better than he. Now Saul watched that prophecy unfold before his eyes. He knew David was the chosen one. Rather than submit to God's plan, Saul determined to kill David—throwing spears at him in the palace, sending soldiers to his house, hunting him across the wilderness with thousands of men.

David's Men — A band of about six hundred who had gathered to David during his years as a fugitive. Scripture describes them as men in distress, in debt, and bitter in soul. David became their captain. They lived with him in the wilderness, sharing his danger and his caves.

The Wilderness of En Gedi — An oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea, surrounded by barren desert. A canyon runs westward from the sea, fed by a spring that creates an unexpected paradise of waterfalls and vegetation in the midst of desolation. The rocky hillsides are honeycombed with caves. The area was known as "the Crags of the Wild Goats" for the ibex that made their home in its cliffs. It was remote, defensible, and full of hiding places—exactly what a fugitive needed.

Saul had just returned from fighting the Philistines when he received word: David was hiding at En Gedi. He gathered three thousand of Israel's best soldiers and went to hunt him down.

While searching the area, Saul entered a cave to relieve himself—not knowing that David and his men were hiding in the recesses of that very cave. David's men saw this as divine providence. "This is the day of which the LORD said to you, 'Behold, I will give your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as it shall seem good to you.'"

David crept forward in the darkness. Saul was completely vulnerable. One thrust of a sword and the years of running would be over. David would be king.

But David did not kill Saul. He cut off a corner of Saul's robe—and even that troubled his conscience. He returned to his men and rebuked them: "The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, the LORD's anointed, to put out my hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the LORD."

David understood something his men did not. Saul was still the LORD's anointed—set apart by God for the throne, even if God had rejected him. The kingdom belonged to God, and God alone could give it and take it away. David would not seize by violence what God had promised to give him.

The passage begins with Saul unknowingly walking into the cave where David waits.

Reading

After Saul returned from pursuing the Philistines, he was told, "David is in the Desert of En Gedi." So Saul took three thousand able young men from all Israel and set out to look for David and his men near the Crags of the Wild Goats. He came to the sheep pens along the way; a cave was there, and Saul went in to relieve himself. David and his men were far back in the cave. The men said, "This is the day the LORD spoke of when he said to you, 'I will give your enemy into your hands for you to deal with as you wish.'" Then David crept up unnoticed and cut off a corner of Saul's robe.

Afterward, David was conscience-stricken for having cut off a corner of his robe. He said to his men, "The LORD forbid that I should do such a thing to my master, the LORD's anointed, or lay my hand on him; for he is the anointed of the LORD." With these words David sharply rebuked his men and did not allow them to attack Saul. And Saul left the cave and went his way.

Then David went out of the cave and called out to Saul, "My lord the king!" When Saul looked behind him, David bowed down and prostrated himself with his face to the ground. He said to Saul, "Why do you listen when men say, 'David is bent on harming you'? This day you have seen with your own eyes how the LORD delivered you into my hands in the cave. Some urged me to kill you, but I spared you; I said, 'I will not lay my hand on my lord, because he is the LORD's anointed.' See, my father, look at this piece of your robe in my hand! I cut off the corner of your robe but did not kill you. See that there is nothing in my hand to indicate that I am guilty of wrongdoing or rebellion. I have not wronged you, but you are hunting me down to take my life. May the LORD judge between you and me. And may the LORD avenge the wrongs you have done to me, but my hand will not touch you. As the old saying goes, 'From evildoers come evil deeds,' so my hand will not touch you.

"Against whom has the king of Israel come out? Who are you pursuing? A dead dog? A flea? May the LORD be our judge and decide between us. May he consider my cause and uphold it; may he vindicate me by delivering me from your hand."

When David finished saying this, Saul asked, "Is that your voice, David my son?" And he wept aloud. "You are more righteous than I," he said. "You have treated me well, but I have treated you badly. You have just now told me about the good you did to me; the LORD delivered me into your hands, but you did not kill me. When a man finds his enemy, does he let him get away unharmed? May the LORD reward you well for the way you treated me today. I know that you will surely be king and that the kingdom of Israel will be established in your hands. Now swear to me by the LORD that you will not kill off my descendants or wipe out my name from my father's family."

So David gave his oath to Saul. Then Saul returned home, but David and his men went up to the stronghold.

Discussion Questions

  • David had the opportunity to take what was rightfully his by force. When have you been tempted to take a shortcut to something you knew was eventually coming?
  • David felt convicted just for cutting Saul's robe—even though Saul was trying to kill him. What does that level of integrity reveal about how David saw authority?
  • Saul wept, acknowledged David was right, and then went back to hunting him later. When has doing the right thing not changed your circumstances?
  • David had been anointed as a teenager but didn't become king until he was 30. What does it take to stay faithful when the timeline doesn't make sense?
  • "I won't touch the Lord's anointed." What boundaries have you set for yourself even when breaking them would be easier?
