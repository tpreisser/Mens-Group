Week 8

Pre-Reading Context

Elijah — The prophet from Tishbe who had just witnessed the greatest victory of his ministry. On Mount Carmel, fire fell from heaven, the people declared "The LORD, he is God," and the false prophets were executed. Rain returned after three years. Yet within hours of this triumph, Queen Jezebel sent word that she would kill him by the next day. Elijah fled in terror—first to Beersheba, then a day's journey into the wilderness, where he collapsed under a broom tree and asked God to take his life. An angel fed him twice, and in the strength of that food he traveled forty days and forty nights to Horeb, the mountain of God.

Mount Horeb — Another name for Mount Sinai, the mountain where God had appeared to Moses in the burning bush and later gave the Law to Israel. This was the most sacred ground in Israel's memory—the place where God had made covenant with His people. Elijah's journey to this mountain was no accident. He was returning to the place where God had revealed Himself to Moses, perhaps hoping for a similar encounter.

Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha — Three men God would use to complete the work Elijah thought had failed. Hazael would become king of Syria and bring judgment on Israel. Jehu would become king of Israel and destroy the house of Ahab and the worship of Baal. Elisha would succeed Elijah as prophet. Through these three, God would accomplish what Elijah could not accomplish alone.

The Seven Thousand — The faithful remnant in Israel whom God had preserved—all who had not bowed the knee to Baal or kissed his image. Elijah believed he was the only one left who served the LORD. God revealed that He had been at work all along, preserving thousands whom Elijah knew nothing about.

Elijah arrived at Horeb and lodged in a cave. The word of the LORD came to him with a question: "What are you doing here, Elijah?" The prophet poured out his complaint: he had been jealous for the LORD, but Israel had forsaken God's covenant, torn down His altars, and killed His prophets. "I, even I only, am left," Elijah said, "and they seek my life, to take it away."

God told Elijah to go out and stand on the mountain, for the LORD was about to pass by. What followed was a theophany—a visible manifestation of God's presence. A great and powerful wind tore the mountains and shattered rocks, but the LORD was not in the wind. After the wind came an earthquake, but the LORD was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake came fire, but the LORD was not in the fire.

Then came something else entirely. The ESV calls it "the sound of a low whisper." When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his cloak and went out to stand at the entrance of the cave. He knew he was in the presence of God.

This is where the passage begins—with God's question, Elijah's despair, and the revelation that would change everything.

Reading

There he went into a cave and spent the night. And the word of the LORD came to him: "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

He replied, "I have been very zealous for the LORD God Almighty. The Israelites have rejected your covenant, torn down your altars, and put your prophets to death with the sword. I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me too."

The LORD said, "Go out and stand on the mountain in the presence of the LORD, for the LORD is about to pass by."

Then a great and powerful wind tore the mountains apart and shattered the rocks before the LORD, but the LORD was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the LORD was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake came a fire, but the LORD was not in the fire. And after the fire came a gentle whisper. When Elijah heard it, he pulled his cloak over his face and went out and stood at the mouth of the cave.

Then a voice said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

He replied, "I have been very zealous for the LORD God Almighty. The Israelites have rejected your covenant, torn down your altars, and put your prophets to death with the sword. I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me too."

The LORD said to him, "Go back the way you came, and go to the Desert of Damascus. When you get there, anoint Hazael king over Aram. Also, anoint Jehu son of Nimshi king over Israel, and anoint Elisha son of Shaphat from Abel Meholah to succeed you as prophet. Jehu will put to death any who escape the sword of Hazael, and Elisha will put to death any who escape the sword of Jehu. Yet I reserve seven thousand in Israel—all whose knees have not bowed down to Baal and whose mouths have not kissed him."

Discussion Questions

  • Do you think there's a version of "going through the motions" that we as guys are really good at hiding? Like, what does it actually look like when a man is physically present but completely checked out?
  • Do you have a script you run on autopilot -- a rehearsed thing you tell yourself or tell other people about where you're at -- that you haven't actually reexamined in a while? What is it?
  • Have you ever had something genuinely meaningful happen to you -- a conversation, a moment, something that should have hit you -- and it just... didn't get through? What do you think was going on?
  • What do you think is more dangerous for a man -- the dramatic crash where everything falls apart, or the slow, quiet season where you're just kind of numb and nobody notices? Why?
  • When you're in a stuck place, do you think you actually need more time thinking about it, or do you think you need somebody to just hand you something to go do? What would a practical next step look like for you right now?
  • Have you ever been so isolated -- even if you were technically around people -- that you started believing stuff about your situation that just wasn't true? What was the lie you were telling yourself?
  • What would it actually cost you to let somebody in right now -- not in a big dramatic way, but just to honestly tell one person where you're really at?
