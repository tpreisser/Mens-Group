Week 4

Pre-Reading Context

The Father — A wealthy landowner with servants, livestock, and an estate large enough to divide between two sons. In this parable, he represents God. His actions would have shocked Jesus' original audience: granting a premature inheritance request, running through the village to embrace a returning disgrace, and throwing a lavish celebration for a son who had wished him dead. The father is the central figure—his grace is the point of the story.

The Younger Son (The Prodigal) — The son who demanded his inheritance early. In Jewish culture, this was equivalent to saying "I wish you were dead." He liquidated his share of the family estate, left for a distant country, and squandered everything on reckless living. When famine struck, he ended up feeding pigs—an unclean animal to Jews—and was so desperate he envied what the pigs ate. He returned home rehearsing a speech to ask for a position as a hired servant. The father's embrace interrupted him before he could finish. By the time our passage begins, he has been restored: given the father's best robe, a ring signifying authority, sandals marking him as family not servant, and a feast in his honor.

The Older Son — The one who stayed. He never left, never squandered anything, never brought public shame on the family. He was in the field working when his brother returned. He comes home to find music, dancing, and celebration—and learns it is all for his brother. His response reveals something about his heart that years of faithful service had concealed.

The Tax Collectors and Sinners — The crowd gathered around Jesus when he told this parable (Luke 15:1). They were the social and religious outcasts of Israel—people who had wandered far from God and knew it. They represent the younger son.

The Pharisees and Scribes — The religious leaders who triggered this parable by grumbling, "This man receives sinners and eats with them" (Luke 15:2). They were outwardly obedient, religiously devoted, and offended that Jesus welcomed obvious sinners. They represent the older son.

Discussion Questions

  • The older brother had been keeping score for years—what he did right, what he never got. What's on your mental ledger?
  • He said "this son of yours" instead of "my brother." When have you distanced yourself from someone because of resentment?
  • The father said "all that is mine is yours"—the older son already had access to everything. He just never took it. Where might you be grinding for something that's already available?
  • The older brother ended up outside the party by choice. Bitterness kept him out, not the father. What does resentment actually cost you over time?
  • What would it take to put down the ledger?
