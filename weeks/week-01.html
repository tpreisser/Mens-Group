Week 1

Pre-Reading Context

Elijah — His name means "My God is Yahweh." A prophet from Tishbe in Gilead who appeared suddenly during Israel's darkest spiritual era. He had spent the previous three years in hiding, sustained miraculously by God—first by ravens at a brook, then by a widow in Zarephath where he raised her son from the dead. He is the man who just called fire down from heaven.

Ahab — King of the northern kingdom of Israel. Scripture says he did more evil than any Israelite king before him. He built a temple and altar to Baal in Samaria and allowed his wife to systematically persecute the prophets of the LORD.

Jezebel — Ahab's wife, a Phoenician princess from Sidon. Her father Ethbaal was both king and priest of Baal. She didn't just permit Baal worship—she funded it, feeding 450 prophets of Baal and 400 prophets of Asherah at her royal table while hunting down and killing the prophets of Yahweh. Her threats were not empty.

The 450 Prophets of Baal — These were not innocent men. They were false prophets who served a false god, leading Israel into idolatry and away from the LORD who had delivered them from Egypt and made covenant with them. Under Jezebel's sponsorship, they had become the official religious leaders of the nation while true prophets of Yahweh were hunted and killed. They stood in direct opposition to God and actively turned His people toward worship of a deity that did not exist.

Israel had been in severe drought for over three years. This was God calling His people back to Himself—demonstrating that Baal, whom they worshiped as the storm god who controlled rain and fertility, had no power at all. Only the LORD gives and withholds rain. Only the LORD sustains life.

Then came Mount Carmel. Elijah stood alone against the 450 prophets of Baal before all Israel. The challenge: each side prepares a sacrifice, but neither lights the fire. The God who answers by fire is the true God.

The prophets of Baal cried out from morning until evening. They danced. They cut themselves until blood poured out. Nothing. Baal could not answer because Baal was not real.

Elijah rebuilt the LORD's altar, drenched it with water three times, and prayed a simple prayer. Fire fell from heaven and consumed everything—the offering, the wood, the stones, even the water. The people fell on their faces declaring, "The LORD, he is God; the LORD, he is God!" The false prophets who had led Israel astray were executed in accordance with God's command in the Law (Deuteronomy 13:1-5)—the righteous judgment for those who turn God's people toward false gods. Then Elijah prayed and the rain came, ending three years of drought as God restored what He had withheld.

The hand of the LORD came upon Elijah, and he outran Ahab's chariot seventeen miles to Jezreel.

Total victory. God had revealed Himself. His people had returned to Him. The false prophets were judged.

This is where chapter 19 begins—immediately after.

Reading

Now Ahab told Jezebel everything Elijah had done and how he had killed all the prophets with the sword. So Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah to say, "May the gods deal with me, be it ever so severely, if by this time tomorrow I do not make your life like that of one of them."

Elijah was afraid and ran for his life. When he came to Beersheba in Judah, he left his servant there, while he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness. He came to a broom bush, sat down under it and prayed that he might die. "I have had enough, Lord," he said. "Take my life; I am no better than my ancestors." Then he lay down under the bush and fell asleep.

All at once an angel touched him and said, "Get up and eat." He looked around, and there by his head was some bread baked over hot coals, and a jar of water. He ate and drank and then lay down again.

The angel of the Lord came back a second time and touched him and said, "Get up and eat, for the journey is too much for you." So he got up and ate and drank. Strengthened by that food, he traveled forty days and forty nights until he reached Horeb, the mountain of God. There he went into a cave and spent the night.

And the word of the Lord came to him: "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

He replied, "I have been very zealous for the Lord God Almighty. The Israelites have rejected your covenant, torn down your altars, and put your prophets to death with the sword. I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me too."

The Lord said, "Go out and stand on the mountain in the presence of the Lord, for the Lord is about to pass by."

Then a great and powerful wind tore the mountains apart and shattered the rocks before the Lord, but the Lord was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake came a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire came a gentle whisper. When Elijah heard it, he pulled his cloak over his face and went out and stood at the mouth of the cave.

Then a voice said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

He replied, "I have been very zealous for the Lord God Almighty. The Israelites have rejected your covenant, torn down your altars, and put your prophets to death with the sword. I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me too."

The Lord said to him, "Go back the way you came, and go to the Desert of Damascus. When you get there, anoint Hazael king over Aram. Also, anoint Jehu son of Nimshi king over Israel, and anoint Elisha son of Shaphat from Abel Meholah to succeed you as prophet. Jehu will put to death any who escape the sword of Hazael, and Elisha will put to death any who escape the sword of Jehu. Yet I reserve seven thousand in Israel—all whose knees have not bowed down to Baal and whose mouths have not kissed him."

Discussion Questions

  • What was Elijah's emotional state after his great victory? How does this relate to your own experiences after successes?
  • Why do you think Elijah felt alone despite God's faithfulness? Have you ever felt this way?
  • How did God respond to Elijah in his moment of despair? What does this teach us about God's character?
  • What role did rest, food, and God's presence play in Elijah's recovery? How can you apply this?
  • How does the "gentle whisper" contrast with the dramatic displays of power? What does this say about how God often works?
